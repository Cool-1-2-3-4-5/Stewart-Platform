STEWART PLATFORM MAZE SOLVER - INVERSE KINEMATICS SPECIFICATION
================================================================
Project:  6-servo rotary Stewart platform that tilts a maze to roll a ball to a goal.
Location: WATiMake makerspace, University of Waterloo.
Purpose of this file: a complete, self-contained description of the hardware and
the inverse-kinematics (IK) math, so that a person or another LLM can repeat the
derivation and the numbers without any other context.

Conventions used in this file
  - Plain ASCII. "*" = multiply, "^" = power, "sqrt" = square root.
  - "." between two vectors = dot product. |v| = length of vector v.
  - Vectors are written (x, y, z). Angles in the math are RADIANS unless marked "deg".
  - Lengths are MILLIMETRES (mm).
  - Values marked [UNMEASURED] are placeholders and must be measured on the build.
  - Values marked [CONFIRM] are my best understanding and should be checked.

Sections
  1. System overview
  2. Hardware specifications
  3. Platform geometry (what the IK depends on)
  4. Coordinate frames and sign conventions

 10. Open items / things to measure

1. SYSTEM OVERVIEW
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Data flow:
  Camera (Pi Camera Module 3)
    -> Raspberry Pi 5: OpenCV finds 4 ArUco tags, warps the image to a flat
       300 x 300 px top-down view of the maze, and finds the ball's (x, y).
    -> At startup only: the maze image is thresholded into a 300 x 300
       occupancy grid and BFS computes the shortest path as corner waypoints.
    -> Every frame: an RL policy (not written yet) picks a platform pose.
    -> IK (this document) turns that pose into 6 servo angles.
    -> Servo angles are sent to a microcontroller that drives the 6 servos
       (ESP32 over UDP per the latest plan; Arduino over serial was the earlier
       plan) [CONFIRM which one].

Software files (folder: Software/):
  Computer_vision.py   camera, ArUco, homography, warp, ball tracking loop
  Maze_Analyser.py     one-shot maze snapshot -> 300x300 occupancy grid
  bfs_mapper.py        BFS shortest path -> corner waypoints + directions
  ik_core.py           generic rotary Stewart IK (written for a PAIRED servo
                       layout by default; see section 7.8 to configure it for
                       this build)
  requirements.txt     Pi 5 install notes and pinned packages

3. PLATFORM GEOMETRY (WHAT THE IK DEPENDS ON)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  G1. Six servos, EVENLY spaced around a circle of radius R_b: one every 60 deg.
      There are no servo pairs.
  G2. Each servo body sits radially, so its output shaft points straight out
      from the platform centre (shaft axis is horizontal and radial).
  G3. Therefore each horn swings in a VERTICAL plane that is TANGENT to the base
      circle (the plane containing the tangent direction and straight up).
  G4. All six horns point the SAME rotational direction around the circle when
      horizontal. This file assumes COUNTER-CLOCKWISE when viewed from above
      [CONFIRM]. If they are clockwise, see section 7.9.
  G5. Each rod is EXACTLY VERTICAL at the home pose: every platform joint sits
      directly above its horn tip when all horns are horizontal.
  G6. Joints at both rod ends are ball joints (rods carry only push/pull).
  G7. Servo i drives leg i; platform joint i is the joint above horn i.

Consequences of G5 (derived in section 7):
  - Platform joint radius R_p = sqrt(R_b^2 + a^2). It is not measured separately.
  - Platform joints are rotated counter-clockwise from the servos by
    delta = atan2(a, R_b).
  - Home height h0 = s (the rod length).

Measured inputs needed (only three):
  R_b  centre -> servo shaft axis, measured horizontally   [UNMEASURED]
  a    horn length: shaft axis -> horn ball-joint centre   [UNMEASURED]
  s    rod length: ball-joint centre -> ball-joint centre  [UNMEASURED]
Measure joint CENTRES, not part edges.

4. COORDINATE FRAMES AND SIGN CONVENTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Base frame {B} (fixed to the ground)
  Origin: centre of the servo-shaft circle, at the height of the shaft axes.
  +z: straight up.
  +x: horizontal, pointing from the centre toward servo 1.
  +y: horizontal, 90 deg counter-clockwise from +x when viewed from above
      (right-handed frame).

Platform frame {P} (fixed to the moving platform)
  Origin: centre of the six platform joints.
  Axes: identical to {B} at the home pose.
  The six joints lie in the plane z = 0 of {P}.

Servo numbering
  Servo i (i = 1..6) sits at angle theta_i = (i - 1) * 60 deg, measured
  counter-clockwise from +x viewed from above. Servo 1 at 0 deg, servo 2 at
  60 deg, ..., servo 6 at 300 deg.

Pose (the IK input), all relative to the home pose
  x, y, z         translation of the {P} origin [mm]
  phi             roll  = rotation about +x [rad]
  theta           pitch = rotation about +y [rad]
  psi             yaw   = rotation about +z [rad]
  Positive angles follow the right-hand rule.
  Rotation order: R = Rz(psi) * Ry(theta) * Rx(phi)
  (equivalent to: roll first, then pitch, then yaw, all about the fixed base axes).

Horn angle (the IK output, before conversion to a servo command)
  alpha_i = angle of horn i above horizontal [rad].
  alpha_i = 0 means horizontal. alpha_i > 0 means the horn tip is raised.

Not yet defined [CONFIRM]: which maze side (tag ID) lies along +x. This is
needed to turn "ball should roll toward +x in the camera image" into a roll or
pitch sign. Record the angle from +x (servo 1) to the Right tag (ID 2).

6. DERIVATION WORKSHEET (HINTS AND CHECKS, NO ANSWERS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 0. Draw two pictures.
  Top view: six servos at 60 deg spacing, each horn along the tangent (all
  counter-clockwise), and each platform joint directly above its horn tip.
  Side view of one leg looking along the radius: horn at angle alpha, rod up
  to the joint.

Step 1. Write theta_i and b_i.
  Hint:  b_i lies on a circle of radius R_b at z = 0.
  Check: |b_i| = R_b; the six points form a regular hexagon.

Step 2. Write u_i and t_i.
  Hint:  t_i is u_i rotated 90 deg counter-clockwise in the horizontal plane.
  Check: t_i . b_i = 0, |t_i| = 1, t_i has z = 0.

Step 3. Write p_i, R_p and delta.
  Hint:  the rod is vertical at home, so joint i has the same x and y as horn
         tip i at alpha = 0. That tip is b_i + a * t_i.
  Check: all six |p_i| are equal.

Step 4. Find h0.
  Hint:  at home the horn tip is at z = 0 and the rod is vertical.

Step 5. Write Rx, Ry, Rz and R = Rz * Ry * Rx.
  Check: R = identity when all angles are 0; R keeps vector lengths.

Step 6. Write q_i and l_i.
  Hint:  q_i = T + R * p_i, l_i = q_i - b_i.
  Check: at home, l_i = a * t_i + h0 * k.

Step 7. Write H_i(alpha).
  Hint:  the horn swings in the plane spanned by t_i and k.
  Check: |H_i - b_i| = a for all alpha; at alpha = 0 it is b_i + a * t_i.

Step 8. Write the rod constraint |q_i - H_i| = s and square it.

Step 9. Rearrange into M * sin(alpha) + N * cos(alpha) = L.
  Hint:  M involves the z part of l_i; N involves the part of l_i along t_i.

Step 10. Solve for alpha.
  Hint:  harmonic addition / auxiliary angle: write M sin + N cos as a single
         sine of (alpha + something), then use arcsin.
  Check: no solution when |L| > sqrt(M^2 + N^2); choose the branch that gives
         alpha = 0 at home.

Step 11. Convert to a servo command and clip to 0..180 deg.

7. REFERENCE SOLUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Servo positions
  theta_i = (i - 1) * pi / 3                     i = 1..6
  u_i     = (cos theta_i, sin theta_i, 0)
  b_i     = R_b * u_i

7.2 Horn direction (counter-clockwise tangent)
  t_i = (-sin theta_i, cos theta_i, 0)
  k   = (0, 0, 1)

7.3 Platform joints (in {P})
  p_i   = b_i + a * t_i
        = (R_b cos theta_i - a sin theta_i,  R_b sin theta_i + a cos theta_i,  0)
  R_p   = |p_i| = sqrt(R_b^2 + a^2)
  delta = atan2(a, R_b)
  Equivalently p_i = R_p * (cos(theta_i + delta), sin(theta_i + delta), 0).

7.4 Home height
  At home: H_i = b_i + a * t_i (z = 0) and q_i = p_i + h0 * k.
  q_i - H_i = (0, 0, h0), whose length must equal s, so
  h0 = s

7.5 Rotation
  Rx(phi)   = [ 1      0          0        ]
              [ 0      cos phi   -sin phi  ]
              [ 0      sin phi    cos phi  ]

  Ry(theta) = [ cos theta   0   sin theta ]
              [ 0           1   0         ]
              [ -sin theta  0   cos theta ]

  Rz(psi)   = [ cos psi  -sin psi   0 ]
              [ sin psi   cos psi   0 ]
              [ 0         0         1 ]

  R = Rz(psi) * Ry(theta) * Rx(phi)
  Written out (c = cos, s = sin):
    R = [ c_psi c_th   c_psi s_th s_phi - s_psi c_phi   c_psi s_th c_phi + s_psi s_phi ]
        [ s_psi c_th   s_psi s_th s_phi + c_psi c_phi   s_psi s_th c_phi - c_psi s_phi ]
        [ -s_th        c_th s_phi                       c_th c_phi                     ]

7.6 Leg vector and rod equation
  T   = (x, y, h0 + z)
  q_i = T + R * p_i
  l_i = q_i - b_i

  Horn tip:  H_i(alpha) = b_i + a * (cos(alpha) * t_i + sin(alpha) * k)

  Rod constraint:
    |q_i - H_i|^2 = s^2
    |l_i - a (cos(alpha) t_i + sin(alpha) k)|^2 = s^2

  Expand. t_i and k are perpendicular unit vectors, so the squared horn term
  equals a^2:
    |l_i|^2 - 2a cos(alpha) (l_i . t_i) - 2a sin(alpha) (l_i . k) + a^2 = s^2

  Rearrange into M sin(alpha) + N cos(alpha) = L with
    M = 2 a (l_i . k)   = 2 a l_iz
    N = 2 a (l_i . t_i) = 2 a ( -sin theta_i * l_ix + cos theta_i * l_iy )
    L = |l_i|^2 + a^2 - s^2

7.7 Solving for alpha
  Let rho = sqrt(M^2 + N^2) and gamma = atan2(N, M).
  Then M sin(alpha) + N cos(alpha) = rho * sin(alpha + gamma).
  (Check: rho sin(alpha + gamma) = rho sin(alpha) cos(gamma) + rho cos(alpha) sin(gamma),
   with rho cos(gamma) = M and rho sin(gamma) = N.)

  So sin(alpha + gamma) = L / rho, and
    alpha_i = asin(L / rho) - gamma

  Reachability: if |L / rho| > 1 there is no real solution. The pose is
  unreachable; reject it and hold the previous command.

  Branch: the other solution, alpha = pi - asin(L / rho) - gamma, puts the horn
  near vertical at home and is never used.

  Home check: l_i = a t_i + s k, so |l_i|^2 = a^2 + s^2, l_iz = s, l_i . t_i = a.
    M = 2 a s,  N = 2 a^2,  L = 2 a^2
    rho = 2 a sqrt(s^2 + a^2),  L / rho = a / sqrt(s^2 + a^2)
    asin(a / sqrt(s^2 + a^2)) = atan(a / s) = atan2(2a^2, 2as) = gamma
    alpha_i = 0   (correct)

7.8 Servo command
  cmd_i = neutral_i + dir_i * alpha_i * 180 / pi      [deg]
  cmd_i = clip(cmd_i, 0, 180)
  neutral_i: command that makes horn i horizontal [UNMEASURED, ~90].
  dir_i:     +1 if increasing the command raises the horn tip, else -1.
             All horns turn the same way, so all six should share one sign
             [CONFIRM on hardware, one servo at a time, with the rods off].

  Configuring ik_core.py for this build (it defaults to a paired layout):
    import numpy as np
    from ik_core import StewartIK
    th = np.arange(6) * np.pi / 3
    ik = StewartIK(base_radius=R_b, horn_length=a, rod_length=s,
                   platform_radius=np.hypot(R_b, a),
                   base_angles_rad=th,
                   platform_angles_rad=th + np.arctan2(a, R_b),
                   horn_plane_rad=th + np.pi / 2,
                   servo_direction=(1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1))
  ik_core.py computes h0 itself; it should come out equal to s. Its formula is
  identical to 7.6-7.7 with t_i = (cos beta_i, sin beta_i, 0), beta_i = theta_i + 90 deg.

7.9 If the horns actually point CLOCKWISE
  Use t_i = (sin theta_i, -cos theta_i, 0) and delta = -atan2(a, R_b).
  Everything else is unchanged. (In ik_core.py: horn_plane_rad = th - pi/2 and
  platform_angles_rad = th - arctan2(a, R_b).)

7.10 Small-angle approximations (useful for checks and for the RL action design)
  For small motions, alpha_i ~= (change in height of joint i) / a, because the
  rod is vertical and the horn is horizontal at home.
  Joint i in {B} at home: (R_p cos(theta_i + delta), R_p sin(theta_i + delta), s).
    Heave z:       alpha_i ~= z / a                              (all equal)
    Roll phi:      alpha_i ~= ( R_p sin(theta_i + delta) / a ) * phi
    Pitch theta:   alpha_i ~= ( -R_p cos(theta_i + delta) / a ) * theta
    x, y, yaw:     alpha_i ~= 0 to first order (see section 9)

8. TEST VECTORS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Example geometry (NOT the real build; used only to check an implementation):
  R_b = 100 mm,  a = 25 mm,  s = 150 mm,  horns counter-clockwise.
Derived:
  R_p   = sqrt(100^2 + 25^2) = 103.078 mm
  delta = atan2(25, 100)     = 0.24498 rad = 14.036 deg
  h0    = 150 mm

Test A - home pose (0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)
  Expected: alpha_i = 0 for all six.

Test B - heave z = +3 mm, everything else 0
  For every leg: l_i = a t_i + (s + 3) k
    |l_i|^2 = 25^2 + 153^2 = 625 + 23409 = 24034
    L = 24034 + 625 - 22500 = 2159
    M = 2 * 25 * 153 = 7650
    N = 2 * 25 * 25 = 1250
    rho = sqrt(7650^2 + 1250^2) = sqrt(60085000) = 7751.45
    L / rho = 0.278529
    asin(0.278529) = 0.28228 rad
    gamma = atan2(1250, 7650) = 0.16197 rad
    alpha_i = 0.12029 rad = 6.892 deg   (all six equal; exact solver value,
                                         hand rounding above gives 0.1203)
  Small-angle estimate: 3 / 25 = 0.120 rad. Matches.

Test C - roll phi = +2 deg (0.034907 rad), everything else 0
  Small-angle prediction: alpha_i ~= (R_b sin theta_i + a cos theta_i) / a * phi
  Exact solver (section 7):              small-angle estimate:
    servo 1 (theta   0): +0.03491 rad  +2.000 deg     +0.0349 rad
    servo 2 (theta  60): +0.13880 rad  +7.952 deg     +0.1384 rad
    servo 3 (theta 120): +0.10363 rad  +5.938 deg     +0.1035 rad
    servo 4 (theta 180): -0.03491 rad  -2.000 deg     -0.0349 rad
    servo 5 (theta 240): -0.13878 rad  -7.952 deg     -0.1384 rad
    servo 6 (theta 300): -0.10363 rad  -5.937 deg     -0.1035 rad
  Pattern: servos on the +y side rise, servos on the -y side drop, by equal and
  opposite amounts.

Test D - unreachable pose, e.g. z = +40 mm (more than the horn length 25 mm)
  Expected: |L / rho| > 1 for at least one leg -> solver reports unreachable.

Test E - yaw psi = +2 deg, everything else 0
  Exact solver: alpha_i = +0.00173 rad = +0.099 deg for all six.
  A 2 deg yaw moves the servos only 0.1 deg (second-order effect only), which
  is why yaw cannot be controlled or resisted at home (section 9).

Test D exact result: all six legs unreachable.

9. KNOWN LIMITATIONS OF THIS GEOMETRY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - With all six rods exactly vertical at home, the rods can only push up and
    down. Small x, y and yaw motions barely change any rod's height, so the IK
    returns alpha ~= 0 for them and the servos cannot hold the platform against
    those motions (it is a singular configuration at home). The owner has
    chosen to accept this. For maze tilting, command only roll, pitch and
    (optionally) z; keep x = y = psi = 0.
  - Pose limits are not yet set. Suggested starting envelope for testing:
    |phi|, |theta| <= 10 deg, |z| <= 5 mm. Raise only after checking for
    mechanical interference.
  - Servo limits: commands are clipped to 0..180 deg. Clipping silently
    changes the pose, so the controller should prefer poses well inside it.
